Remote, but from where?

The pitch

Problem. "Remote" on a job post usually means "remote within a country", and the location field is free text. On the live board right now there are … spellings for remote engineering locations ("Remote, USA", "Remote (US)", "US Remote", "United States (Remote)"…), and … roles say only "Remote". Job seekers outside the US find out after reading the JD, or after applying. I lived this over the last two weeks: GitLab's agent roles say Remote but hire in four countries, Hugging Face says "EMEA Remote", and aggregators still list roles their ATS already closed.

What I'd build.

  1. Normalize location → eligibility at ingest: countries, regions and time zones a role is open to, with rules for the common spellings and an LLM pass over the JD for the "Remote"-only cases. This demo runs a rule-based version of the JD pass when you click Check the JD. On the run I tested, rules alone settled about two in five of the "Remote"-only roles from phrases like "fully remote within the United States" or "open to candidates based in the US, UK and Canada". The rest is where an LLM earns its keep.
  2. Ask once, filter everywhere. One profile field ("I'm in X, I need / don't need a visa") that the jobs board, /api/v1/jobs and the MCP server all respect, so an agent searching for a candidate only returns roles they can take.
  3. Freshness. Re-check each role against its ATS's public API (Ashby, Greenhouse, Lever) so closed roles drop off fast.

Why it moves your goals. Fewer dead-end applications builds the trust you say earns the right to point people at speedrun roles. It makes the network worth joining for the large pool of international engineers. And it makes the MCP server more useful to agents, which can't eyeball a JD the way a person does.
